Group Roles & Responsibilities

In the project, to give each person a task that is both fair and best for each person, we split all the tasks based on what each person was comfortable with. We divided between the members with more experience with HTML/CSS development and those who preferred to complete the research and writing. This helped make sure that each member completed their part without any issue.

Dawid built the HTML for the website, brushing up and making sure each page matched the same format. Fagan handled the CSS to provide a matching and simple design, while ensuring the pages still function as required. For the research, Adam covered AI tools used for climate action for SDG 13, Ross covered the history and background of AI, Cairn and Fagan looked into how AI affects education for SDG 4, and Arthur looked into the environmental impact of data centers.

Planning & Intermediate Milestones

To ensure our website was completed in good time, we had to make sure to evenly distribute our roles and to set out milestone targets to keep on top of work so that we were able to move onto the next part of developing our website. Initially, we were hoping to have our individual content and webpages complete by the 12th of October, which we went slightly over by a couple of days but still had finished within plenty of time to allow us to proofread and put it all together into the website's final state.

Since the milestone and planning stage at the start of developing our website, we also decided that we would each take on a higher word count to allow other members of the team to write less and focus more on the development of the website itself. We found this extremely effective in improving the quality of our website, given the previous experience that some of us had working with HTML and CSS already.

Ensuring Effective Teamwork

To ensure effective teamwork, our group of six split into multiple teams. We had two topics and the website to create, so working in teams allowed us to motivate each other to get the work done and also ensure we did not overlap in our research and put the same thing. We kept ourselves aware of the deadline, which allowed us to work in a structured, non-rushed manner. We made sure to constantly correct any unseen errors in our work and to exchange ideas amongst ourselves to achieve the best possible result, while also keeping a positive, engaging environment.

Decision-Making Process

At the start of our project, we had a look through all of the Sustainable Development Goals with a lot of consideration for which ones we wanted to select to write our project on. The two that stood out the most between all of us were Quality Education and Climate Action, as they seemed like the most relevant to the current age of generative AI. We thought these were the best two because of the rapidly changing and advancing pace of AI's use in the average day. We also took time to decide which role everyone in the group would have, making sure it was something they felt they would enjoy researching or an area they felt most comfortable working on.

Reflection & Lessons Learned

To make sure we all effectively completed the work, we separated the work evenly across the group. Two people would work on each topic, one worked on the homepage and intro, and another worked on the HTML and homepage. This was to ensure that the work was completed efficiently.

We all had equal say in the decision-making process, with members being able to choose what part of the website they wanted to make. Decisions on the process were decided as a group, which meant that everyone was aware of what was going on and was not left confused or behind.

One area we could improve on as a group is making sure work is not done at the last minute. Too often as a group, we completed work on the day before or the day of the deadline, which resulted in unnecessary stress.
