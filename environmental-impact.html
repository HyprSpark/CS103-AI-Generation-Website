Introduction

Over the past few years, the use of generative AI has vastly increased in the average persons day-to-day life. People use it for asking a quick question, generating creative content, or analysing data. But a lot of people don’t consider what goes on behind the scenes when they are using these tools, and the immense amount of energy that is required to power these AI agents.

Energy Consumption

some data centers can use as much energy as a small city and with generative AI being very resource hungry and relying heavily on fossil fuels the number of daily power consumption and emissions can be extremely harmful for the environment

The tremendous use of fossil fuels leads to tons of greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide and methane) being released into the atmosphere which means heat from the sun is trapped in the atmosphere raising the global average temperature and accelerating climate change

The heightened global average temperature also leads to glaciers melting in places like antarctica meaning sea levels rise which can cause problems such like extreme weather, flooding, land loss, and can also harm ecosystems and animal habitats.

Water Usage

AI data centers also require a lot of water. Previous data centers would use air cooling systems, but now AI requires water cooling since the data centers are much more energy intensive.

“A medium-sized data center can consume up to roughly 110 million gallons of water per year for cooling purposes, equivalent to the annual water usage of approximately 1,000 households. Larger data centers can each “drink” up to 5 million gallons per day, or about 1.8 billion annually, usage equivalent to a town of 10,000 to 50,000 people.” (Yáñez-Barnuevo, 2025). [26]

This can pose as a problem for many different reasons including, local water depletion leading to potential damage to local ecosystems, and also the fact that a lot of the water used in these data centers evaporate or become contaminated meaning it can’t be repurposed.

Hardware and Electronic Waste

Generative AI also has a detrimental effect on the environment during the manufacturing of hardware. The Nvidia A100 GPU was build using various common and rarer elements, the common elements included copper, iron, tin, silicon, and nickel, though other rarer metals were used “Four of the eight precious metals— gold, silver, platinum, and palladium—were detected, though primarily in trace amounts” (Falk, Kluge Corrêa, Luccioni, Biber- Freudenberger, van Wynsberghe, 2026) [7]

“Mining and processing these minerals significantly affect the environment, through high energy demand and associated emissions, groundwater depletion, water and soil contamination, deforestation and soil erosion, in turn contributing to biodiversity loss, land degradation and harming human health.” (Reitmeier, Lutz, 2025) [20]

And with AI being on such a rise currently it means that the demand for these materials are only going to increase meaning the environment will be even further harmed by these processes.

Effects on Animal Habitats and Ecosystems

With the AI tools requiring massive data centers a cost of constructing these is having to clear space for them to be build. This could be forests, grasslands, wetlands or farmlands which means destroying wildlife habitats and dividing ecosystems making it hard for animals to find food and a new place to live (Bisambhar, 2026). [3]

When AI centers are being constructed that can mean more traffic during the building phase and also increased noise pollution which would make things much harder on the local wildlife to find places to inhabit and also disrupt nocturnal animals like bats, hedgehogs and badgers.

However, there can be some potential positives for wildlife conservation as AI algorithms can automate and accelerate the time taken for data analysis in wildlife, therefore research can be undergone for certain species in different habitats and also if any issues start to arise with invasive species in those habitats meaning action to prevent invasive species running out the native wildlife can be taken sooner rather than later.

Of course, with this there are risks including the possibility that AI can misidentify a species, which can be extremely fatal if an endangered species is being monitored (Mammal Society, 2026). [14]

AI e-waste is expected to become a huge problem in coming years if the progression and demand of AI continues at its current rate. E- waste comes from old devices and electricals being thrown away which generally contain harmful toxins or chemicals that can harm the environment. It is expected that “Depending on the adoption rate of generative AI, the technology could add 1.2 million to 5 million metric tons of e-waste in total by 2030” (Crownhart, 2024). [5] This would have a harsh effect on the environment surrounding the e-waste as the metals can soak into the ground from rainfall damaging soil by disrupting the soils chemistry and fertility.

Summary

Overall, the environmental impact of generative AI is far more complicated than most people assume. From the enormous demand for energy and water needed to run data centers, to the manufacturing of specialised hardware, AI currently has very negative effects on ecosystems and wildlife throughout the construction and running of the tools.

As demand for generative AI increases, these problems will only become more abundant, especially with the vast amounts of e-waste being created by AI and the ongoing need for rare minerals. However, AI also has the potential to support wildlife conservation and monitoring when used in a beneficial way.

This means in the future the plan might not be to entirely abolish AI, but to ensure it is manufactured and deployed in a way that reduces harm to the environment and supports sustainable practices. By investing in more environmentally friendly options for AIs development, the damage to the environment caused by AI can be managed more effectively in the future.
