How Generative AI Developed

So, what is the main intent for generative AI and what are its roots? Well, an exact date cannot be pinpointed for when AI was first invented; however, there are many key indicators as to what may have led to the development of generative AI as we know it today. In 1906, a Russian mathematician called Andrey Markov created a method for statistics to predict the behaviour of processes for machine learning. This was used as a fundamental building block in the development of word prediction and autocomplete that we have developed and integrated as a normal function now used on all our laptops, smartphones, etc. [24]

Psychologist Frank Rosenblatt from the University of Cornell developed the first known network that was capable of being trained. This was developed in 1957 and failed due to its process being overly time-consuming. [8]

These are both examples of what could be considered as the first ideas and directions headed in for the construction of generative AI. However, the first computer chatbot was created by Joseph Weizenbaum between the years of 1964 and 1966, which was designed to respond to a user in an empathetic and human manner. This was a groundbreaking creation for its time; however, it was limited due to its inability to understand information and more so the use of its ability to respond to a given input. AI today can compute and calculate what it is given and learn to help it become smarter, but this was just the start. [21]

Generative AI Today

So, let's fast forward to the current day and think about the AI as we now know it. One of the most popular and well-known generative AI platforms, ChatGPT, was first released to the public for use in late November 2022. This followed its first public statement about the platform 4 years prior in 2018, which stated that OpenAI’s main duty of care was for humanity rather than stakeholders/investors, etc. The company wanted to make it clear that the purpose is not intended to harm or negatively impact humans in any way and that it is a breakthrough for modern technology for us to learn how to use it and make it aid us to do the mundane, time-consuming tasks that we spend most of our time on. It was reported that by 2023 OpenAI was generating revenue of $200 million and popularity was only increasing. In 2024 OpenAI announced the development of GPT-5, expanding the AI’s capabilities and moving it forward in ways that scientists like Andrey Markov back in the 50s would never have expected. It has come a long way since, and it shows. Users can now get the chatbot to analyse pictures, generate pictures, and have conversations with it as if it is a real human. [16]

Risks and Limitations

Although the progress that this new development is making has been exponential and the tools that we can now use sound greatly beneficial to us, there are precautions that we need to take, and ways that we can still go about our lives without the use of AI that need to stay the same. In June of this year 2026, an OpenAI bot hacked into an Australian website operated by the government. This happened during a test where the bot was supposed to "look up answers, and available statistics for questions about Australia during an internal evaluation"; however, it was months after that evidence of the hack was discovered. The attack was minor and didn’t pose any real threat, but it does make us question whether we need to be more careful of what we teach this new AI that we are developing and what we should really be using it for. [15]

Environmental Impacts

There are many ways in which we may not have considered that generative AI impacts us, and we have learned most of these through experiences that I have mentioned. However, there are some on the horizon that are becoming increasing issues not only for us as humans but for our planet, that we were not quite so sure on before this became such a big topic. Companies such as OpenAI all use massive amounts of water to cool their systems and infrastructure due to how much energy it uses and the heat that the servers generate as users across the world bombard it with demands. This has caused an uproar from activists and climate experts saying we can’t keep doing it this way and we need to put our planet and its resources first. Protesters gathered outside the Scottish government recently to voice their concern on local data centres disrupting the nation's energy and water supply.

Moving Forward

For the future of AI, we can only assume that it is going to become more powerful and more intelligent by the day. It is up to us to use it wisely and to our advantage. In a recent statement from OpenAI, they have said that they want to ease users into the use of generative AI and that increased usage and practice with their tools should help us for good. This works given that users can all agree on a way that their AI should be used and how it can help us sensibly and benefit our society as we focus on struggles such as economic issues and more global issues like climate control, etc. It puts us in a position where instead of being forced to choose a side between the anti-AI activists and the scientific experts that develop these money-making powerful machines, instead, we must move forward with it. Choosing to integrate it into our lives positively and learn how we can let it help us rather than turn against us. [1]
